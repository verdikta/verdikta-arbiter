## Claims with no verdict

20 of the 39 claims that reached the jury have no verdict. Every juror is required, so one juror's failure leaves the arbiter abstaining. What a juror that did answer said is shown for reading only: it is no settlement, and it never would be one.

In all 20, the DeepSeek call stopped at its 8,000-token limit. In 14, reasoning took the whole limit and the reply was empty. In 6, a reply began and was cut off, and the repair call could read no scores from it. Reasoning counts against that limit, and this juror runs at effort high. Of its 48 calls, 21 ended at the limit. OpenRouter sent the calls to nine different hosts, and the limit was reached on seven of them. Every call to the Claude juror finished inside the limit, at 6,184 output tokens at most. The External Adapter retries only a timeout, so these claims get no second call. Measuring what this panel can judge once it answers needs a decision from the owner about the limit, the effort or the juror, so nothing here was changed to find out.

## Stated caveats

- `spec.repo` names the contest's GitHub organisation, so a juror may recognise a public finding. That could flatter agreement.
- Rejected claims argue the bug in a hunter's voice on purpose, and the reasons for their labels are in a field the jury does not read.
- Every panel here is one arbiter's single evaluation: run-to-run variation and the spread across arbiters are not measured.
