Severity classes: Verdikta's definitions, version 2, for the rubric immunefi-v2.3, after the Immunefi Vulnerability Severity Classification System v2.3 (https://immunefi.com/immunefi-vulnerability-severity-classification-system-v2-3/). The listing binds deployed contracts and their source: judge the consequence for the deployment and its users. Value taken or locked is classed by that consequence, whoever brings it about (an attacker, another account, or the contract's own code with no one attacking it) and whoever holds it (a user, a depositor, a fee recipient or the protocol). Funds or NFTs are locked when their holder can neither withdraw, redeem nor move them. A lock is for good when it cannot end without an upgrade of the code or an owner or admin stepping in to release it (changing a setting, calling a rescue function); it is for a period when it ends without that: after a time, at a later step of the protocol's normal flow such as its next report, or once the activity that renews it stops. Judge an amount at the size the proof of concept shows: an amount worth less than the gas of the call that moves or mints it, such as one wei per call, counts as nothing taken, lost, owed or minted, whichever class it would otherwise meet. Classify by the consequence the proof of concept demonstrates, or that follows from it by a concrete line of reasoning, which the claim sets out, tied to a file and line; a finding takes the highest class whose definition that consequence meets.
- CRITICAL: Assets are lost or locked for good. The proof of concept shows one of these, brought about, with no privilege the scope excludes, by an attacker, another account or the contract's own code: principal that users or the protocol hold in the contracts, or that moves through them, taken from those it belongs to (fee code that mints far more shares than are due takes every holder's principal by dilution); NFTs taken from the users who own them; funds or NFTs locked for good; assets minted, or what an NFT represents changed, without authority; principal or NFTs won through a source of randomness that can be foreseen or biased; a governance decision made to carry out something other than what was voted; or the protocol left owing more than it holds.
- HIGH: Value is taken or frozen, short of losing principal for good. The proof of concept shows one of these, brought about by an attacker, another account or the contract's own code: yield or royalties that have accrued but not been claimed, taken from those they are due to, as when the code pays an account more yield than it is owed out of what pays everyone's; such yield or royalties locked for good; or funds or NFTs locked for a period, whoever holds them, a depositor or a fee recipient as much as any other user.
- MEDIUM: The contract is harmed but no value is taken. The proof of concept shows someone able to harm others without taking value, and the harm done to them: stopping the contract working as specified for others (for example by leaving it without the token balance it needs, or by filling blocks so that a time-bound call cannot be included in time); harming users or the protocol with no gain to themselves, such as by making another user's own call revert; making users pay gas they should not have to; or making the gas a call needs grow without bound. When nothing is taken or locked, a failure the code has by itself, which the proof of concept does not show anyone turning on others, is Low.
- LOW: Something is broken but nothing is lost. The proof of concept shows the contract not doing what it promises or documents, with every user's principal intact and no one shown turning the failure on others, for example: it credits a lower return than it states, the difference staying in the contract (a return not delivered, not yield taken or locked); a function answers wrong, or reverts where it should answer; it refuses a configuration, a kind of signature or a deposit it should accept; or a restriction or check does not hold as documented, too strict or too loose. A finding whose only effect on value is an amount worth less than the gas of the call that moves or mints it is Low too.

Outcomes, in this order: INVALID, DUPLICATE, LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH, CRITICAL.
- INVALID: not a finding. The behaviour the proof of concept asserts is not required of the code, the proof is not faithful, or a gate failed.
- DUPLICATE: the root cause is a listed known issue or a finding already valid on this listing, even when the entry calls the behaviour intended or accepted.
- LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH, CRITICAL: a valid finding of that class.
